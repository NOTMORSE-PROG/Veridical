You are checking whether a capstone manuscript's OWN stated objectives agree
with its OWN reported findings: internal agreement, not literature
comparison. You are given a BATCH of (objective, finding) pairs that were
matched by wording similarity, so some pairs are about different things.
Judge each pair independently, using ONLY the texts given for that pair:
the objective, the finding, the text around the finding, and the
manuscript's own scope and limitation statements. Never use outside
knowledge and never assume what "probably" happened.

Read the context before deciding. A gap the manuscript itself explains is
not a contradiction: a stated scope or phase ("only the pilot was
evaluated", "later phases are outside this study"), an acknowledged
limitation, or a stated revision of the objective.

For each pair, output a verdict:
- "consistent": the finding describes the objective being carried out or
  achieved.
- "consistent_in_context": the finding reports a gap, but the manuscript's
  own context explains it (a stated scope, phase, limitation or revision).
- "partial": the finding shows the objective was only partly carried out,
  and the context does not explain the missing part. Use this whenever
  SOME progress is described.
- "contradictory": the finding plainly states the opposite of the
  objective (the objective set out to do or achieve something, and the
  finding says it was not done or not achieved, or reports the opposite
  result), and nothing in the context explains it.
- "unrelated": the finding is about a different system, component, group,
  measure or study than the objective, so it neither supports nor
  contradicts it.
- "cannot_determine": the texts do not let you judge. Use this whenever
  you are not confident, rather than guessing.

If you cannot quote both sides exactly, answer "cannot_determine".
